Cost moves the other way

What W actually is

W is an artificial per-turn cap on the assembled prompt, enforced by the assembler before the request is sent. The model's real context window stays at 262,144 tokens in every arm, so the provider never rejects anything and no result is an artifact of a model limit.

How it is enforced (policies.mjs → evictToBudget):

avail = W − head(498) − reserve(512)
keep  = anchors (last A=4 units, never evicted)
        + units in rank order while they still fit avail

Three things come out of the same budget, and only the third is negotiable:

componentsizeevictable?
frozen head (system 132 + task 366)498 toknever
reply reserve512 toknever — held back for the answer
context unitsW − 1010yes — what eviction fights over

That fixed tax is why a tight cap bites harder than it looks: at W=4,700 head+reserve consume 21% of the budget, leaving 3,690 tokens of actual context; at W=9,500 the same tax is only 11%, leaving 8,490. Halving the cap cut usable context by 57%, not by 50%.

Units, and what is NOT comparable

W and the achieved peak are counted with the harness estimator estTokens = characters ÷ 4, not the provider's tokenizer. total_prompt_tokens is the provider's real count. They are consistent within an experiment but are not interchangeable, so W is not directly a provider-token budget.

Enforcement check

Achieved peak lands 519 and 529 tokens under the cap at W=4,700 and W=9,500 — the reply reserve being held back, as designed — and cap_violations = 0 in every cell. Predicted peak is W − reserve = 4,188 / 8,988; observed 4,181 / 8,971, the small shortfall being discrete unit sizes that cannot fill the budget exactly.

Reading the curve

  • Dose–response is steep: 4,700 → 46% · 5,500 → 67% · 6,500 → 33% · 7,500 → 100% · 8,500 → 100% · 9,500 → 100% · ∞ → 100% (truncate-tail only). Adjusted for arm, the odds ratio on achieved peak is 42× per e-fold.
  • Cost moves the opposite way: 273,290 → 632,225 prompt tokens across the same span. Capping is cheaper and worse — the operating point is a trade, not an optimum.
  • The curve is not monotone point-to-point: W=6,500 (1/3) sits below W=5,500 (2/3). With n=3 at the interior levels that is within noise, so the cliff is located no better than 5,500–7,500.
  • W is a proxy for ACHIEVED PEAK, which is the real variable. Given achieved peak, nominal W adds nothing (LR χ²(1)=0.010, p=0.92) and neither does eviction cadence (p=0.97). See report-cadence-confound.md.
  • One problem (C0). This curve is for longbuild, not for agentic coding in general.
