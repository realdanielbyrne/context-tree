DV3 — eviction cadence & cache TTL

A small window does pay for its own re-caching — if you evict infrequently. Simulated with the repo's ProviderCacheSimulator. Generated 2026-09-14.

There is an interior optimum eviction cadence. Evicting every turn is the worst policy (−183%); evicting every ~25 turns makes a capped window 33.5% cheaper than append-only. The earlier conclusion that "append-only is cache-optimal below the window" was an artifact of testing only cadence N=1 and generalising from it.

Savings by cadence

Mutation is charged per mutation; the read discount accrues per turn. Evicting every N turns amortises one write across N cheaper turns — crossover at N·r·(C_large − C_small) > w·S.

The mechanism

At N=1 the bar is almost entirely cache-write; by N=50 it is almost entirely cache-read. The optimum sits where the two trade off.

Resumption

A cold cache punishes whoever holds the most context — which is append-only. Its cache-write nearly triples across the resumption boundaries while the capped arms barely move, so the small window's advantage grows from +26.3% to +39.4%. This is the regime long sessions actually live in.

Tier dependence

g* = w/r is not the constant 12.5. At the 1-hour write tier (2.0×), cadence-5 flips from +5.2% cheaper to −23.5% more expensive — pricier writes mean you must evict less often. The optimum stays ~25. Re-derive per tier rather than hardcoding.

What this does NOT establish

Simulated, not live. No model was run — this is the current best cost model, not a measurement. And cost says nothing about task success: the cheapest cadence may evict content the task needs. Pair with the live arm before promoting anything.
  • Synthetic uniform session (constant head, constant tokens/turn); real sessions are bursty.
  • Eviction drops the oldest units — the maximally cache-destructive choice, since it invalidates the entire prefix after the head. Dropping late-position units would be cheaper but those are the most relevant. Unexamined, and likely where the next gain is.
  • TTL is modelled as a hard cold start at the resumption turns, which is what an expired cache does to the next request.
